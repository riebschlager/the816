Many people have a story about how something they posted went viral. Hearing these stories can feel like someone describing their dreams. "So it was your mom, but NOT your mom? Ok. And she didn't have a face? But you still knew it was her? Wow... Cool."

#### But This Will Be Totally Different

As a sign of good faith, dear reader, I will compress the boring "virality story" parts into a bulleted list:

- I started the year with around 1,000 Instagram followers
- Today I have nearly 20,000
- My content has earned over 5.5 million views in the last 90 days
- What have I been posting? [I dunno. You tell me.](https://www.instagram.com/riebschlager)

#### That's Out of the Way, Now the Good Stuff

Instagram has been moving from a follower-centric social network towards a recommendation-centric media system. You could make the argument that Instagram was never really a social network, and that's a whole mess we can get into in another post. But for today, the important fact to remember is this: **The content you are served is guided less by who you follow and more by what systems think you want to see.** And if you've ever lost entire parts of your day to short form video content, you know how often these systems can get it right.

#### And That's Bad...Right?

Listen, I'm not going to defend a multi-billion dollar enterprise that profits from harming users. But I am about to make an argument for why I believe algorithmic recommendation can be an amazing tool to connect artists and audiences. This post is my attempt to find a gem or two amongst this big ol' pile of crap.

So, strap on those rubber gloves and let's get digging.

#### Hi. I'm a Niche Artist

Here's the thing: I post weird shit. I've always loved chaotic and colorful visuals. I remember seeing MTV for the first time at a friend's house. (We didn't get cable until I was in high school and even then, no MTV) It wasn't just the music videos, it was also the visual language of the bumpers and interstitials that absolutely cracked my brain open.

I've spent my whole adult life trying to make things that match that same energy. I'm a mediocre artist, but I'm a slightly-above-average programmer. So naturally, creative coding frameworks like [Processing](https://processing.org/) and [openFrameworks](https://openframeworks.cc/) gave me a platform to express myself visually through code.

Making and posting my experiments has become a near-daily art practice for me. I couldn't tell you why I feel compelled to do this. It's just...fun?

It's led to a lot of fun opportunities. I've been invited to do live concert visuals, motion-reactive LED walls for parties, outdoor projected art installations, SO much fun stuff.

But I've always had a modest following online. And that's totally fine, I sincerely don't care. So it really threw me for a loop when suddenly I went from 5 likes per post to tens of thousands.

This is where the wave started for me. This is essentially a gif I found of Ned Flanders that I added some video feedback effects to in TouchDesigner. On Instagram, you'd hear Chemical Brother's "Block Rockin' Beats" under this.

Is this video objectively better than the thousands of things I posted before it? Certainly not. But something different happened. It started finding people who liked it. Those people liked other things I made.
